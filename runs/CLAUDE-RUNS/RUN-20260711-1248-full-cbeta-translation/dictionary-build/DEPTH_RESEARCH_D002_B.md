## 1. The staff (拄杖子)

### Sense verdict

Keep one corpus-wide sense: **the staff**, the handled walking staff that becomes a teaching-seat implement. The 27,727-hit base family (拄杖) and the suffixed form (拄杖子; current research count 4,111) name the same object. The suffix does not produce another referent.

The corpus bends the object aggressively: a master raises, plants, hands over, or speaks through it; it becomes a dragon, swallows the world or Mount Sumeru, speaks a teaching, distinguishes those who recognize it, and is made the subject of naming prohibitions. Those are distinct deployment classes of the same staff. They do not justify separate “dragon,” “cosmos,” “speaker,” or “samadhi” senses. This differs from the staff/blow split required for the stick or stroke (棒): the present evidence continues to handle, name, give, take, break, plant, and contemplate an object rather than count a separate act of striking.

### Definition-formula inventory

- Direct baseline: “the staff is an ordinary implement used by a patched-robed monk” (拄杖子是衲僧尋常用底). This is the clearest object-level grounding.
- Ostension: “this is a staff” (這箇是拄杖子), followed by picking it up, holding it, and moving it. This confirms the handled object.
- Open question: “what is the staff?” (如何是拄杖子) occurs in Blue Cliff commentary, but proposed answers such as somersaulting or clapping are rejected. It is an interview question, not a stable lexical definition.
- Plain-naming rule: “on seeing a staff, simply call it a staff” (見拄杖子但喚作拄杖子).
- Naming bind: “it must not be called a staff, and it must not fail to be called a staff” (不得喚作拄杖子，不得不喚作拄杖子). This tests naming the object; it does not introduce a second thing.
- Recognition formulas conflict deliberately: Fenyang says recognizing the staff completes the matter of travel (識得拄杖子行脚事畢); Sanjiao says recognizing it sends one to hell like an arrow (識得拄杖子入地獄如箭); later comments add “wrong.” These cannot be collapsed into a doctrinal gloss.
- No stable allowlisted “X is Y” formula was found that replaces the physical staff with a second referent. Metaphorical equations such as “this staff is samadhi” remain local public deployments.

### Deployment inventory

1. Ordinary implement of the patched-robed monk.
2. Teaching-seat handling: picked up, raised, planted, leaned, held across, or put down.
3. Give/take public case: possession and deprivation in Bajiao's saying.
4. Physical transfer and breakage: a monk receives the staff and snaps it in two.
5. Staff as responder or speaker: it can “distinguish” or teach equality day and night.
6. Dragon transformation and swallowing heaven and earth.
7. Reciprocal swallowing with Mount Sumeru.
8. Cosmic handling: picking it up, holding it, and moving it shakes the great-thousand world.
9. Collective compression: buddhas, patriarchs, and old masters become the master's staff.
10. Recognition formulas and their explicit counter-formulas.
11. Plain naming and the positive/negative naming bind.
12. Ostensive interview: a monk calls the displayed object a staff and is rejected.
13. Local equation with samadhi and recognition of the old masters.
14. Staff versus staff-head family: the object remains distinct from the location or point denoted by “staff-head” (拄杖頭), though the latter is a related consumer.

### Proposed 14-anchor allocation

All fourteen belong under the single object sense. They exceed the keystone floor without repeating one gesture class.

### Recommended definition repair

Retain the present one-sense architecture, but rebuild depth around the fourteen classes above. Lead with the concrete walking staff and teaching-seat implement. Then state where Zen bends it: it becomes a public-interview object, named and unnameable, giver and taker, respondent and teacher, dragon and world-swallowing implement. Do not define any of those transformations as what the word secretly “means.” Preserve the explicit return to “just one staff.”

## 2. Master / ordination preceptor (和尚)

### Sense verdict

Keep two senses because the corpus assigns the title to two different institutional relations:

1. **teaching master / community head** — the named person who teaches, presides, receives questions, is quoted, or is addressed as “Master”; and
2. **ordination preceptor** — the human conferrer from whom someone receives precepts, explicitly distinguished from teachers, tonsure masters, and other biographical roles.

Direct address is not a third sense. “Master” in a vocative question and “the master” in narrative prose can name the same teaching person; capitalization and English syntax change, not the referent. Likewise, “hall-head master” (堂頭和尚) is an explicit office within the first sense, not automatically a third thing.

### Definition-formula inventory

- Allowlisted “Master X, who was ...” headings (X和尚者) open biographies, for example Bodhidharma and Puhua. They identify titled persons and then narrate origin or lineage; they are not lexical definitions of the headword.
- A lexical etymology of 和尚 appears in non-allowlisted Vinaya commentary, but it fails the governing source gate and must not enter the entry as evidence.
- The teaching sense is established by relational and institutional formulas: “Master X said” (X和尚曰), “please, Master, speak the teaching for the assembly” (請和尚為眾說法), “hall-head master” (堂頭和尚), and direct questions addressed to the master.
- The ordination sense has stronger role formulas: “received the precepts from Preceptor X” (受戒於X和尚), “asked Master X to be the preceptor from whom the precepts were received” (乞X和尚為得戒師), and the title “preceptor from whom the precepts were received” (得戒和尚).
- Bare “Precept Master” (戒和尚) is unsafe as a detector. In “Wuzu Jie, the master” (五祖戒和尚), 戒 is the personal name Jie. Exact false-positive control: `X/X80/X80n1565.xml` 0243a10–0243a11, 到五祖戒和尚處。祖問曰。不落唇吻一句。作麼生道。 This is a visit to Master Wuzu Jie, not an ordination preceptor.

### Deployment inventory

Teaching-master sense:

1. Named title in a lineage heading.
2. Biographical “Master X, who was ...” opening.
3. Master-disciple provenance: someone comes from or studied under a named master.
4. Direct address requesting compassion or instruction.
5. Direct question to a publicly encountered master.
6. Request that the master speak for the assembled community.
7. Quoted authority: “Master X said.”
8. Institutional community head: “hall-head master.”
9. Narrative address to the master during illness or before death.
10. Collective “old masters” compressed in a public saying.

Ordination-preceptor sense:

1. Sequential biography separates tonsure, investigation/study, reception of precepts, and hearing the teaching.
2. Another biography contrasts hearing teaching under one teacher with receiving precepts from another preceptor.
3. Explicit appointment as “preceptor from whom the precepts were received.”
4. Incense verse coordinates the precept-conferring preceptor with two separate tonsure masters and invokes the three collections of precepts.

### Proposed 14-anchor allocation

Allocate ten anchors to the high-frequency teaching-master sense and four to the independently attested ordination-preceptor sense. Four is sufficient for the smaller sense because the witnesses preserve four non-duplicate relational facts; the entry as a keystone still carries fourteen anchors overall.

### Recommended definition repair

The existing two-sense split is corpus-proven and should remain. Rebuild the teaching-master sense to at least ten non-padding anchors spanning heading, biography, lineage provenance, public interview, teaching-seat request, quotation, office, illness/death address, and collective use. Keep direct address inside that sense and explain the English rendering rule rather than duplicating the sense.

Keep the ordination-preceptor sense at four high-information anchors unless research uncovers a genuinely new relation. Its definition should lead with the human conferrer of ordination and explicitly contrast the teaching and tonsure roles shown by the corpus. Continue rejecting bare (戒和尚) as sufficient evidence because personal-name Jie creates high-frequency false positives.
