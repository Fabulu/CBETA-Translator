## L002-A item-8 ledger and full retest (2026-07-13)

- sense-target-distinguishability: MERGE “had no reply” and “without verbal phrases” into one clean target, “without words.” Both state absence of words. The longer expression “no verbal phrases” (無語句) supplies “phrases” with the following graph 句; syntax and the human-versus-phrase host do not create a second lexical referent for 無語.
- Family retest: “had no response” (無對), “question-and-answer” (問答), “able to speak” (道得), “verbal phrases” (語句), the dominant “the monk had no reply” formula (僧無語), and Deshan's “our school has no verbal phrases” formula can all remain true under the literal absence-of-words sense. No doctrine of silence is inferred.
- Definition retest: “without words” is the literal umbrella; “had no reply” remains the preferred contextual rendering in public encounters. The former split tracked host and grammar, not two objects, and was removed.
- #0g retest: the entry foregrounds the term's pervasive role as a public-interview stage direction and preserves later masters' quotation and criticism of the no-verbal-phrases statement. It reports the recorded continuations and appraisals without deciding that silence means victory, defeat, realization, or blankness.
- Depth repair: retained all seven non-duplicate deployment witnesses and added an independent public exchange where a monk's lack of reply receives an explicit appraisal. Final depth is eight anchors across seven source texts, meeting the 4,794-hit floor without padding.
