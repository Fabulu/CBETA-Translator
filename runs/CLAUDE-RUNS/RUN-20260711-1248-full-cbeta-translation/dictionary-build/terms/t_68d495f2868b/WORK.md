# Printing block / block-stamped Chan (印板) — research inventory

- Allowlisted concordance: 59 hits in 37 texts.
- Definition formulas: one record asks “what is block-stamped?” and answers that people without genuine understanding rely entirely on ancient sayings as a staff; another explicitly defines “block-stamped Chan” as reading the records of Yuanwu, Dahui, and Zhongfeng and producing something resembling them.
- Deployment classes retained: direct definition, named three-Chan taxonomy, quoted criticism of teachers stamped from a block and released from a mold, literal production of a transmission-record printing block, and an imperial instruction to print texts wherever canon printing blocks exist.
- Sense split: required. The physical printing block used to print books and the Chan criticism of model-stamped speech are different referents; neither exact English target covers both. The Zen-loaded sense is primary.
- Family check: compared mold (模子), block-stamped Chan (印板禪), printed from a block (印板上打來), carved block (刊板), and printing the canon (印藏經). The model/mold collocation supports the technical criticism; the actual book-production passages support the physical sense.
- Omission audit: three anchors preserve the technical definition, independent taxonomy, and widespread block/mold formula; two independent texts anchor the literal object. No class is omitted.

## Gloss-hygiene and family retest

- sense-target-distinguishability: sense 0 `model-stamped Chan speech` names speech or teaching criticized as reproduced from fixed models; sense 1 `engraved printing block` names the physical block used to print a book. Exact definition and production passages prove different referents, so the split stands.
- Item 8: `block-stamped` and `mechanically copied from a model` were two readings fused into one target, not two senses. Replaced them with the single technical-referent label `model-stamped Chan speech`.
- Family retest: 印板禪, 印板上打來, and 模子裏脫出 support the technical criticism; 彫造印板, 刊板, and 藏經印板 support the material object. The two definitions remain simultaneously true only as separate senses.
