## Sense analysis
1. **null / corpus-wide (doctrinal)** — "Buddha-nature," the Nirvana-Sutra 一切眾生皆有佛性. Present and quoted straight (Yanguan), underwrites 見性成佛, but even here bent to level rather than build metaphysics (Huineng: 佛性無南北). Multi-source (Yanguan, Huineng; Guishan states the affirmed premise's shadow).
2. **Zhaozhou Congshen (master-specific = the Chan divergence)** — 佛性 turned into a live checking-word. Asked if a dog has it (the sutra says yes), Zhaozhou says 無, grounding it deflationarily as 有業識性在; says 有 for a cypress; once dodges (家家門前通長安); and demotes 佛性 itself to 貼體衣服，亦名煩惱. The bald 無 becomes THE huatou (Dahui; Wumenguan case 1). Guishan's 一切眾生皆無佛性 is the parallel inversion by a second master.

## Multi-source verdict
**Both senses multi-source.**
- Doctrinal: Yanguan + Huineng + the sutra premise quoted across the record.
- Chan turning-word: Zhaozhou's own record (multiple exchanges) + Guishan's inversion + Dahui's method + the independent 天聖/聯珠-type citations (T48n2021, D48n8939) all carrying 業識 + 無. Independent texts and masters.

## Anti-fakeout note (why this term matters)
Reading the general-Buddhist "innate potential for Buddhahood" INTO Zhaozhou's 無 is exactly the fakeout the guide warns about. The corpus itself blocks it: Zhaozhou answers **有** for a cypress and calls 佛性 **defiled clothing** — he is *using* the word as a lever, not affirming a metaphysical possession. The doctrinal sense is real (sense 1) but Chan's signature move is to refuse to rest in it (sense 2). Kept the two senses cleanly separated rather than collapsing them into a single mystical gloss.
