## Public-feedback and falsification gate

- feedback-observations: Niutou Farong names the action and Daoxin interrogates both observer and mind; the attributed treatise defines and evaluates it; Yongming Yanshou quotes a positive no-mind formula; the Yongzheng Emperor and Xuri Jushi criticize particular deployments; Huineng criticizes the related act of dwelling on mind and observing quiet.
- feedback-inference-verdict: LICENSED — “inspect mind” is the minimal English action supported across affirmative and critical predicates. The corpus does not license silently equating it with modern mindfulness or declaring one global doctrinal verdict.
- feedback-falsification-searches: definition forms, 觀心一法, 觀心無心, 靜觀心, 持心觀心, 觀心誤, 住心觀靜, 看心, title/table-of-contents uses, and the distribution concentration in the Record of the Source-Mirror.
- feedback-counterexamples: affirmative treatise formulas prevent defining the headword as inherently rejected; direct criticisms prevent defining it as uniformly endorsed. Both concern the same act under different predicates, not different referents.
- feedback-scope: the target is corpus-wide; every endorsement or criticism remains explicitly speaker- or text-specific.
- lookup-probes: inspect mind, observe mind, look at mind, contemplate mind, mind observation, watch the mind.
- opening-interpretation-verdict: PASS — the referent appears first, before frequency and textual disagreement.
- nested-compound-verdict: 觀心無心 and 靜觀心 are deployments of inspecting mind, while 住心觀靜 is retained only as labelled contrast evidence.
- verb-frame-verdict: inspect, gather, understand, mis-seek, divide, and criticize all take the same action as their object or subject; no second thing is attested.
- family-definition-retest: every added witness still fits “inspect mind”; the sense remains single after enrichment.
- omission-audit: all Chinese evidence retained in prose now has an exact stored anchor; the removed Linji paraphrase was not needed to establish a class already anchored by named witnesses.
