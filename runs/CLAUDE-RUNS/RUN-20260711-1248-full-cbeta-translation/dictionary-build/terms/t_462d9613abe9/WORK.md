## Image layers

### Sense 1 — a bird's trackless flight-course

1. **Graph composition:** bird + road/course.
2. **Ordinary scene:** a bird crosses open air; unlike a road worn or impressed into ground, its course leaves no fixed track on which the next body can place a foot. This uses only ordinary physical relations plus the corpus's repeated no-track/no-lodging predicates.
3. **Chan deployment / bend:** the records turn that flight-course into a named Caodong member of the Three Roads and put it into public interviews, instructions, answers, appraisals, and later comments. It can be traveled, can receive people, can be called difficult to pass, and can also be explicitly not traveled in Dongshan's original-face answer. These are attested uses, not a prescribed method or symbolic code.

### Sense 2 — a precipitous trail fit for birds

1. **Graph composition:** the same two graphs.
2. **Ordinary scene:** a very high, narrow, or steep mountain trail, grouped with rope bridges and sheep-gut paths and explicitly traversed through danger and mountain ranges.
3. **Chan-corpus deployment:** the referent occurs in memorial/travel narrative and a named master's retrospective of wandering. It is a physical path over mountain ground, not the trackless course through air. The Chan corpus preserves both ordinary topographic use and technical use; no further mystical deviation is assigned to this second referent.

## Rejected and narrowed claims

- **REJECT:** “the path cannot be traveled or followed.” It conflicts with 171 wide-window travel forms and direct human traversal in both senses.
- **REJECT:** “a spiritual path,” “a prescribed procedure,” or any imported method. The corpus supplies a named route/image and encounter uses, not those outside categories.
- **REJECT:** one blurry “bird path” sense. The mountain-travel narratives and trackless-flight predicates require different physical referents.
- **KEEP WITH NARROW SCOPE:** “trackless.” It is licensed for the primary flight-course by several independent predicates, but not imposed on the terrestrial mountain-trail sense.
- **KEEP WITH NARROW SCOPE:** “fit for birds.” It describes the mountain trail's steep/narrow scene, not an assertion that humans cannot traverse it.
