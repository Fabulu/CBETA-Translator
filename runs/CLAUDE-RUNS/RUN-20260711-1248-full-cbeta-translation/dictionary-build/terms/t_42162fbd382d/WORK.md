# Cat (貓兒) — research inventory

- Allowlisted concordance: 458 occurrences in 137 texts. Traditional 貓兒 and variant 猫兒 are separate searchable headwords but the same word; cross-linked, never treated as different senses.
- Sense test: ordinary cats, mouse-catching cats, dead cats, and the animal in Nanquan's case are the same kind of thing. The case is a distinct deployment, not a distinct lexical sense, so this entry remains single-sense.
- Main deployment classes: Nanquan's two halls fight over a cat; Nanquan offers to spare it if the assembly can speak and cuts it when they cannot; Zhaozhou's later shoe response; later inquiry and verdict (“it takes a Nanquan”); ordinary mouse-catching comparison; later precept/killing commentary.
- Passage-level precept result: a late record explicitly says one hall cited “the bodhisattva precepts do not permit keeping cats, foxes, pigs, and dogs,” while the other wanted the cat against mice. The same record calls the killing a sin. This proves an attested later precept framing, but not the intent of the early case. The entry labels chronology and does not decide the dispute.
- Family cross-check: consistent with 南泉斬貓, 斬貓, 兩堂爭貓 and the 猫兒 variant. No separate “kitten” referent is required by the evidence harvested.
- Omission audit: case narrative, later verdict, ordinary animal deployment, and explicit late precept framing are all anchored.
