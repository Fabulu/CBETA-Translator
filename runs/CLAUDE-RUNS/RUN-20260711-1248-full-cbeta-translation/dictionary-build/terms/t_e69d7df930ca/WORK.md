# Jinniu / golden ox (金牛) — research inventory

- Allowlisted concordance: 393 occurrences in 148 texts; the string has several different referents.
- Primary Zen deployment: the named master Jinniu and his meal-call case. Independent lamp and case witnesses record him carrying a rice bucket before the monks' hall, dancing, laughing, and calling, “Bodhisattva children, come eat” (菩薩子喫飯來). Later records abbreviate this as “Jinniu calls [them] to the meal” (金牛喚飯) and “Jinniu's meal” (金牛飯).
- Grouped-case finding: *Guxue Zhe's Record* explicitly places “Nanquan cuts the cat, Guizong cuts the snake, Jinniu calls [them] to the meal, Zhaozhou drinks tea” in one list. The entry reports the grouping and the speaker's own appraisal, without deriving a shared precept meaning.
- Roster boundary: Jinniu is a lineage master and belongs on the master roster. This dictionary article covers the lexical allusion/case shorthand carried by his name, not a substitute biography or roster record.
- Different-thing audit: literal/imaginative “golden ox” uses are a second referent and therefore a second sense. Place names (Jinniu Lake, Jinniu Mountain) and later masters/temples also named Jinniu are proper-name collisions, recorded for disambiguation but not promoted into lexical senses.
- Definition-formula audit: no corpus self-definition of the compound was found. “Jinniu is an elder under Mazu” is biographical identification, not a definition.
- Family cross-check: consistent with meal-family terms 飯, 喫飯 and case abbreviations 金牛喚飯/金牛飯. No passage was found connecting Jinniu's case itself to a precept violation.
- Omission audit: original case, independent lamp witness, later case-family grouping, productive shorthand, and different literal referent are all anchored.
