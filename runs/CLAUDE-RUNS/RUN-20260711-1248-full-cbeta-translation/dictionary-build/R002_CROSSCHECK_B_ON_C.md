## 蟭螟 — CORRECTION REQUIRED

- The one-creature sense, size-reversal inventory, seven-anchor depth, and #0g account are otherwise sound. Nesting on a mosquito's eyelash, containing a giant turtle or night market, draining the ocean, swallowing tiger/Sumerus, and entering a net are predicates involving the same creature.
- The preferred target **“the mosquito-eyelash mite” overstates the corpus**. The exact defining witness calls it 蟭螟蟲 and says it nests on a mosquito's eyelash; it does not identify a zoological mite. “Mite” is an imported taxonomic choice. Use an English-first target such as **“the minute jiaoming insect”**, with **“the mosquito-eyelash insect”** as a descriptive alternate; retain `jiaoming` only to identify the otherwise unnamed creature.
- The explanation's statement that a master calls **“a monk caught in his verbal net”** such a mite is also stronger than the saved KWIC, which says only, “today a jiaoming insect blundered into the net” (今日有箇蟭螟蟲撞入網中). Unless the wider exchange explicitly identifies the entrant as a monk and the net as verbal, revise this to the observable wording: **a master says that a jiaoming insect has blundered into the net**.
- These are translation/evidentiary corrections, not a sense split. The inherited “mythical” and fixed-cosmos readings were correctly rejected.
