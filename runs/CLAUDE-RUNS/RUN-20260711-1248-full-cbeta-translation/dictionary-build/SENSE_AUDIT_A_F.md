# Sense audit: IDs `t_a*` through `t_f*`

Report-only audit under `DICTIONARY_ENTRY_GUIDE.md` depth gate item 8. No entry was edited.

## Scope and method

- Audited all 135 `STATUS=done` term directories whose ID suffix begins `a` through `f`.
- Read every current preferred target, sense key, explanation, and curated occurrence.
- Tested especially for (1) literal object versus Zen-loaded deployment, (2) word/common noun versus title or person, and (3) corpus-wide versus genuinely master-specific referents.
- Ran the semicolon detector over `PreferredTarget`. In this ID range it finds `敗闕`, `腳跟`, `舌頭`, and `垂示`. The other named detector terms (`血脈`, `蹉過`, `現成`, `思量`, `休去歇去`) fall outside this ID range and are not adjudicated here.
- Corpus examples below come only from paths in `Assets/Data/zen-corpus.json`. They are candidate evidence, not authorization to rewrite before root review.

## Confirmed split candidates

### `舌頭` — high confidence

Current single target: **“tongue; capacity to speak.”** This fuses a bodily organ and the faculty/output of speech.

- Bodily organ: `喫飯咬著舌頭` (“while eating, bite the tongue”; `J/J26/J26nB182.xml`, 0453a23), and `適纔吃瓜子，咬破舌頭` (“just now, eating melon seeds, [I] bit the tongue”; `J/J40/J40nB475.xml`, 0051a10).
- Speech organ/capacity as a Chan verdict: `佛法太煞有，只是舌頭短` (“the buddha teaching is very much there; it is only that the tongue is short”; `J/J10/J10nA158.xml`, 0007c07), and the current succession anchor `坐斷天下人舌頭` (“sit across and cut off the tongues of everyone under heaven”), where no injury is narrated.

No single precise English gloss denotes both the bitten anatomical tongue and a master's capacity/reach in public speech. Split, putting the Zen-loaded speech sense first, and anchor each sense.

### `垂示` — high confidence

Current single target: **“an indication; to offer an indication.”** The corpus lexicalizes two different word classes/referents.

- Noun/editorial object: `垂示云。定乾坤句...` (“the indication says: a phrase that settles heaven and earth...”); the Blue Cliff rubric introduces a written case-preface. `室中語要一百八十五則，垂示代語二百九十則` counts “290 indications and substitute answers” as textual items.
- Verb/event: `方便門中，請師垂示` (“within the expedient gate, please, master, offer an indication”), followed by the master's spoken answer.

These are respectively a textual indication/preface and the act of giving one. Item 8 expressly permits a split for a genuinely different word class. Put the Zen editorial noun first if the concordance confirms that it is the more technical deployment.

### `腳跟` — high confidence

Current single target: **“the heel; one's footing, the spot on which one stands.”** It merges anatomical heel with the heavily lexicalized Chan footing/underfoot locative.

- Anatomical heel: `腳跟流血` (“the heel bleeds”; `J/J39/J39nB471.xml`, 0953a29), and `腳跟忽被斧頭傷` (“the heel was suddenly injured by an axe”; `J/J39/J39nB435.xml`, 0004b01). `腳跟痛` (“the heel hurts”; `J/J26/J26nB185.xml`, 0579b12) is another bodily predicate.
- Zen-loaded footing/place underfoot: `腳跟下好與三十棒` (“right under [his] heels, worth thirty blows”), `腳跟不點地` (“the heels do not touch the ground”), and `立定腳跟` (“plant the heels firmly”). These function as verdicts about footing or the place where one stands, not reports of heel anatomy.

Split literal heel from Zen-loaded footing/underfoot, with the latter first. Re-check every existing occurrence because `腳跟下痛與一頓` can look anatomical while grammatically awarding blows “under the heels.”

### `珍重` — high confidence, found beyond the semicolon detector

Current single target: **“take care.”** The corpus also uses the same graph as a transitive verb meaning to value/treasure something.

- Formulaic leave-taking: `久立，珍重` (“you have stood long; take care”) and `珍重將去` (“take care as you go”), already represented by the entry.
- Value/treasure an object: `屢承翰教，珍重法門` (“having repeatedly received your written instruction, [I] treasure the teaching gate”; `J/J26/J26nB177.xml`, 0051b20); `又能珍重此錄，廣為流佈` (“and was able to treasure this record and circulate it widely”; `X/X72/X72n1442.xml`, 0699a15); `若欲珍重其事` (“if [you] wish to value that matter”; `J/J34/J34nB311.xml`, 0624c14).

A farewell formula and transitive valuing are different acts with different syntax. Split and anchor both; the distinctively Chan leave-taking formula should remain primary.

### `頌古` — medium-high confidence

Current explanation explicitly calls it both **“the act, and the resulting literary genre”**, but keeps one sense.

- Compositional act: `作頌古百首` (“made one hundred verses on old cases”) and `作頌古` (“compose verses on old cases”).
- Countable work/genre section: `有拈古焉有頌古焉` (“there is picking up old cases; there are verses on old cases”), headings and named sets such as `雪竇頌古百則` (“Xuedou's hundred verses on old cases”).

This is not two interpretations of one poem: it is the act of versifying versus the resulting verses/genre. Confirm from clean KWICs whether the bare nominal examples denote the works rather than elliptical composition, then split if confirmed.

## Merge/keep adjudications from the semicolon detector

### `敗闕` — keep one sense; remove semicolon ambiguity in later editing

“Failure” and “exposed fault” paraphrase one verdict, not different things. The constructions `納敗闕` (“incur failure”), `一場敗闕` (“an episode of failure”), `敗闕處` (“place of failure”), and the direct question `某甲有什麼敗闕處` (“what failure do I have?”) vary the grammar around the same adverse result in an exchange. No second referent was found. Prefer one precise target (probably “failure”); treat “exposed fault” as explanatory language only if evidence warrants it.

## Additional review candidates (do not rewrite without more concordance work)

- **`小參`**: the entry combines the institutional convocation with a textual/genre heading (`卷第八　上堂八　小參一`). This may be ordinary metonymy—the heading indexes records of the same event—rather than a new thing. Review alongside `上堂` and other event-as-section headings before splitting.
- **`結制`**: target is the event/verb “begin the restriction period”; headings or addresses delivered on that occasion may merely label the same event. Review, but current material does not yet prove a different referent.
- **`作家`**: the current “adept” is strongly attested in `作家相見` (“adepts meeting”). A raw wider-canon “author/householder” reading is not enough; no allowlist proof collected here establishes a distinct ordinary author sense. Keep pending a targeted allowlist search for unambiguous authorship/household-management grammar.
- **`無生`**: “unborn” can predicate a person, name non-arising, or occur in fixed compounds such as `無生忍`. These may be grammatical shapes of one non-arising referent rather than different things. Do not split until passages force two non-vague English glosses.
- **`大悟`**: nominal “great awakening” and verbal “awaken greatly” name the same event; word-class conversion alone is not enough when the referent remains identical. Keep one unless a proper title/person is found.
- **`清規`**: the binding institutional code and works titled “Monastic Code” may be a code versus a document bearing that code. Current examples do not establish that the proper title is used independently enough to require a second dictionary sense. Review bibliographic occurrences before deciding.
