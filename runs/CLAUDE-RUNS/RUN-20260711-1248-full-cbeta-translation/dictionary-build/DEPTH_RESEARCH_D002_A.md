## 禮拜 — `t_1d3473614976`

### Definition and sense audit

The current definition, “to bow / to prostrate,” still holds. The evidence consistently denotes the ritual bodily action. Master, monk, lay commander, and emperor are different actors, not different things; bowing before a question, after an exchange, before being struck, or before withdrawing are narrative deployments, not separate senses.

The six existing witnesses already cover awakening followed by a bow, a challenged bow, a monk bowing before a strike, a master bowing and being questioned, a monk bowing and withdrawing before recall, and a commander bowing and taking a teacher. Add three non-padding deployments for a total of nine:

1. **Imperial ceremonial actor** — `B/B25/B25n0144.xml`, `0550b04–0550b05`

   > 皇帝便禮拜。皇帝又別問

   The emperor bows and then asks a separate question. This establishes courtly/ruler deployment without changing the action sense.

2. **Return to the assembly in public-interview choreography** — `J/J37/J37nB383.xml`, `0210c07`

   > 僧禮拜歸眾。乃云：「喜鵲簷前噪

   The monk bows and returns to the assembly before the master continues.

3. **Bodily sequence: bow, rise, and act** — `B/B25/B25n0144.xml`, `0570a07–0570a08`

   > 禮拜起來，收他一隻履

   This explicitly supplies the rising phase after prostration and the next prescribed action.

### Family and #0g decision

Keep the English-first gloss concrete and ritual: “to bow; to prostrate oneself.” Surface that in encounter records the bow is part of public-interview choreography and may precede a question, blow, withdrawal, recall, or acceptance of a teacher. Do not infer submission, awakening, agreement, irony, or defeat unless the record states it.

Repeated `便禮拜` formulas and further copies of bow-and-withdraw sequences would be padding. No sense split is warranted by actor, rank, or consequence.
